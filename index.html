05 / QUICK START

Use BirdieLab well

A repeatable setup makes your practice clips easier to compare. This version records video and stores shot data you enter; it does not yet calculate launch metrics automatically from the camera.

01

Set the phone safely

Use a stable tripod or phone clamp. Keep the phone outside the hitting area and well clear of the club path. Never balance it on a golf bag or place it where a ball or club could strike it.

02

Choose a consistent view

For a useful swing/ball-flight clip, start with a side-on view: place the phone several feet to the side of the ball, perpendicular to the target line, with the lens around hand-to-waist height. Frame the ball, clubhead and the first part of the flight. Adjust distance and framing for your space.

03

Keep the picture clean

Use bright, even light; avoid pointing directly into the sun. Clean the lens, use the rear camera, keep the phone still, and avoid digital zoom. Keep the target line and background uncluttered.

04

Record one shot at a time

Select the club, start the camera, record a short clip, then stop recording. Review the clip before the next shot. Enter only measurements from a trusted launch monitor or other reliable source in the shot form.

05

Build comparable data

Use the same ball, tee height, camera position and practice conditions where possible. Log mishits too, and add a short note about strike or shot shape. Export your CSV periodically to back up your log.

About phone placement

There is no single camera position that captures every launch variable. Side-on is a practical starting angle for reviewing launch direction and early flight. A future automated measurement system will need a defined camera geometry, calibration and testing against a trusted launch monitor before its numbers can be treated as measurements.
